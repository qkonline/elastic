# Elastic

A planner where **the order of the list is the plan**, with habits, quarterly goals and weekly reviews around it. It was called Elastic Day until it grew beyond the day.

You add what you want to do and roughly how long it takes. You never type a start or end time: they're worked out from the order of the list, from anything pinned to a fixed time, and from what actually happened. Run a task long, pause it, push it to later, and the rest of the day moves to fit.

Live at **[kaiserkhan.com/planner](https://kaiserkhan.com/planner/)**. It works on a phone or a desktop, in light or dark, installs like an app, and keeps working offline.

## How it works

- **Your day, your hours.** On a first visit, two questions shape the plan: when your day usually starts (or that it varies) and how long a productive day is for you. With a start that varies, the day's length counts from when you press _Start my day_. Change either in Settings → _Your day_.
- **Tasks, buffers and fixed times.** A task has a duration: pick a preset or set your own hours and minutes. A buffer is slack you can't start. A fixed item (a meeting, say) sits at its time and everything else flows around it. Put more in front of it than fits and it moves later, showing the time it was set for; a timer running over doesn't move it, you get a warning instead. The New task sheet explains each type in a line under the choice, and each has its own mark (a dot, a dashed ring, a clock). Give each one of five colours, or any colour you like.
- **See how the day is going.** Once you've started, the top of the day shows when you started and when you're likely to be done, with a line that fills in as the day goes.
- **The day at a glance.** A strip at the top of the day shows every task as long as it takes, in its colour: done is faded, the running task fills as it goes, idle time is hatched, and a line marks now. Above it, when the day started and when it's likely done.
- **Start my day, then one timer.** Tasks can be started once you've pressed _Start my day_ (until then the plan is dimmed, but you can still change it). A task counts down; past zero it counts up in red, with a soft two-note chime and a buzz on phones. Starting another task finishes the one that's running (a paused one keeps the time you put in, and the rest of it comes straight after the new one), and whichever task you start moves up to where you are in the list, so everything still to do is planned after it.
- **Checks for things with no duration.** A new task starts with _Timed_ off, as a check: it goes on the day's checklist above the timeline, and you tick it off instead of timing it. Switch _Timed_ on (or pick Buffer or Fixed time) to give it a duration and a place on the timeline.
- **Late nights stay one day.** Once you've started a day, it stays _Today_ past midnight until you press _End my day_ at the bottom of the day, so working until 2 am still counts as the day you started. The wrap-up shows what got done and can move whatever's left to tomorrow. A day you forget to end closes at 4 am, or three hours after a later planned wrap-up.
- **Idle time doesn't move the plan.** If nothing is running, planned times stay where they are, so the things you didn't get to show up as _missed_. Reschedule them (start now, later today, another day) or mark them done.
- **Postpone.** Before a task starts, "Later today" moves it to the end of the list. Mid-task, the time you've put in stays logged and the rest becomes a new task, later today or tomorrow.
- **Reorder by dragging.** Drag a task by its colour bar (or press and hold its name on a phone); the other tasks slide aside to show where it will land. The task sheet has Earlier/Later buttons too.
- **Look first, edit on purpose.** Tapping a task shows it: where it sits in the day (picked out of the day's strip), what it's set to, and what you can do now (start, skip, postpone, tick off). Changing it happens behind Edit task, in the same form as New task, and only saves on Update task. Habits work the same way.
- **Repeating tasks.** Every day, weekdays, or one weekday. Editing a repeating task also updates the other days you haven't started yet, before and after the one you edited. Drag one past another repeating task, or delete one, and you choose whether that's just for today or for later days too.
- **Habits.** Add one from New task: something to tick off, an amount to reach (8 glasses of water: each tap adds one, press and hold to type a number), minutes to spend (timed tasks can count towards it), or a habit to break (each day counts as clean until you mark a slip). Every day, on chosen days, or a number of times a week or month, with an optional reminder. Habits sit in the Checklist among your checks, or in a section of their own (Settings), and a habit's sheet shows its streak, how often you've kept it and a month of days. The Checklist is a list that folds away past three entries (the third fading out), or, if you prefer (Settings), a row of chips that scrolls sideways.
- **Day, week and month.** The switch at the top shows one day, a week or a month. The week is a time grid on a computer (each task a block at its time) and, on a phone, seven strips side by side with the chosen day's tasks listed below, with a toggle; in the grid, drag a task to another day or time, or tap an empty slot to add one there. The month shows how full each day is (task names, a bar of time planned and logged and a ring for habits kept on a computer; a small strip of the day's tasks and a line for habits kept on a phone). Swipe or use the arrows to go back and forward a week or a month.
- **Goals and reviews.** The bar at the bottom switches between Today, Habits (every habit's week, streak and today's tick), Goals and Review, each a screen of its own, with Add task in the middle. Goals belong to a calendar quarter: give one a number to reach, milestones, or just the words and why, and link tasks and habits to it so their time and days count. Once a week, at a day and time you choose, Elastic offers a review: the week in numbers (time logged and which tasks it went on, tasks done, habits kept, what kept being moved) and a check-in on your goals. At the end of a quarter it asks how each goal went and lets you set the next quarter's. Past reviews are kept to read back.
- **Other days** are for planning: the week strip lets you look ahead or back and move things between days. On a phone, swipe sideways to go to the next or previous day, and pull down from the top to refresh.
- **Notifications.** The first time you start a timer or give a task a fixed time, Elastic offers to notify you when time's up and a few minutes before fixed times. You can turn that on, leave it for now (it asks again the next day), or tell it not to ask again. There's a switch in Settings either way, and a choice of how early the heads-up comes (5, 10 or 15 minutes, or none). With the app open, the heads-up is a soft chime and a banner at the top.
- **Fix the record.** Forgot to tap Start, or tapped Done long after you'd finished? Open the task and move when it started or finished on a strip of time; the rest of the day follows. Done on a task well over time asks whether you finished when time was up, and the day hours let you move when the day started.
- **Ended days are a record.** Once you've ended a day you can look back at it, but not change or reorder it. Today can be reopened if you ended it too soon.
- **Always the latest version.** When you come back to the app, it checks whether a newer version is out and quietly reloads onto it, so a copy on your Home Screen never falls behind.
- **Sync across devices.** Make an account in Settings → _Sync across devices_ and sign in on your phone and your computer: the same plan on both, and a change on one shows up on the others straight away. You make the account with your email (a six-digit code confirms it) and your name, then add a passkey (Face ID, Touch ID or your device's screen lock) to sign in quickly; a code to your email always works too. There's no password. Changes made offline go up when you're back online, and if two devices change the same day at once, both changes are kept, task by task. Theme, sound and notifications stay set per device.
- **Project cards from Boardo.** Connect [Boardo](https://kaiserkhan.com/boardo/), a shared project boards app, in Settings → _Projects_, and the cards assigned to you there show in a folding _Projects_ section on the days they cover (an overdue one that's in progress stays until it's done). Start one and it counts up with no time limit, sets the card to In progress in Boardo and logs the time you work on it there; Done marks your part done. Starting something else, or ending your day, only sets it aside. The connection belongs to the device it was made on and isn't synced.
- **Install it.** Settings has an _Install_ button in browsers that support it (Chrome, Edge, Samsung Internet), and short instructions for Safari on iPhone, iPad and Mac. Installed, it opens from its own icon in its own window. On iPhone and iPad this is also what makes notifications possible.

## Your data

You don't need an account. Without one, your plan is saved in your browser (IndexedDB), on the device you're using, and never sent anywhere. Settings → _Your data_ exports a backup file and imports it again. Clearing the site's data deletes your plan, and Safari can clear it after a week without a visit unless the app is on your home screen, so export now and then.

With an account, your plan (days, repeating tasks and the settings for how your days work) is also stored with the account on Cloudflare, so your other devices can get it. Each device still keeps its own full copy and works offline. Signing out leaves that copy where it is; deleting the account removes the account's copy and leaves each device's. The account holds your name and email and your passkeys' public keys, and no password. Sign-in codes are emailed through [Resend](https://resend.com).

Notifications while the app is closed (see below) need the push service to know _when_ to ring (your timer running out, a fixed time coming up). Without an account that's all it's told; with one, the account also says what each notification is for, since another device may have changed the plan.

## Development

Needs Node 20.19+ (or 22.12+).

```bash
npm install
npm run dev        # http://localhost:5173/planner/
npm test           # unit and integration tests (Vitest)
npm run check      # type-check the Svelte and TypeScript code
npm run format     # Prettier
npm run build      # production build in dist/
npm run preview    # serve the build at http://localhost:4173/planner/
```

Built with Svelte 5, TypeScript and Vite. The only things shipped besides the app's own code are the Instrument Sans and Space Grotesk fonts and [SimpleWebAuthn](https://simplewebauthn.dev)'s browser helpers for passkeys.

### Layout

```
src/
  lib/
    schedule.ts        works out every item's start and end from the list (the core of the app)
    today.ts           which day counts as Today, including after midnight
    actions.ts         every change to a day as a pure function: start, pause, postpone, reorder…
    planner.svelte.ts  app state: the loaded days, the clock, what's open, saving, notifications
    db.ts              IndexedDB: one record per day, repeating series, settings
    repeat.ts          repeat rules and building days from repeating tasks
    rows.ts            what each timeline row looks like at a given moment
    drag.ts            reordering tasks by dragging
    gestures.svelte.ts swiping between days and pulling to refresh
    backup.ts          export, and checking files on import
    account.ts         making an account and signing in (email codes, passkeys)
    sync.ts            keeping this device's plan and the account's the same
    merge.ts           combining a record changed on two devices, task by task
    push.ts            booking time's-up pushes with the push worker
    install.svelte.ts  the browser's install prompt
  components/          the screens and sheets
  sw.js                service worker: offline cache and showing pushed notifications
worker/                Cloudflare Worker: notification pushes, accounts and sync
public/                icons, web app manifest, .htaccess for the host
scripts/deploy.sh      build and upload the site
```

A few rules the code sticks to:

- Start and end times are never stored. `schedule()` recomputes them on every tick from the order of the list, durations, fixed times and the timer timestamps.
- Times are minutes since midnight of the item's day (570 is 9:30 am), which keeps the scheduling maths simple and lets a running timer survive a reload. A late night keeps counting past 1440, so 1:30 am after a Friday is Friday's minute 1530.
- A day that hasn't been touched isn't stored; it's built from your repeating tasks when you look at it.

## Notifications while the app is closed

A web page can only ring while it's running, and phones pause pages as soon as they're locked or in the background. So when Elastic goes into the background, it books pushes with a small [Cloudflare Worker](worker/) for the moment a running timer runs out and for each heads-up before a fixed time, and cancels them when you come back. The pushes are empty: the app's service worker shows the notification and reads what to say from the browser's own storage. The worker only ever holds a push address and some times.

Signed in, the account does the booking instead: the open device keeps it up to date with what's coming, and when a notification is due, every device of the account with notifications on gets a push, except one that has the app open in front of you (it rings by itself).

Without the worker configured, notifications still work whenever the page is running (on a desktop, that includes background tabs), and there are no accounts.

To run your own:

```bash
node worker/scripts/vapid-keys.mjs                # prints a key pair
# put the public key in worker/wrangler.toml (VAPID_PUBLIC_KEY) and .env.production (VITE_VAPID_PUBLIC_KEY)
cd worker                                         # Wrangler needs Node 22+
npx wrangler secret put VAPID_PRIVATE_KEY         # paste the private key
npx wrangler d1 create elastic-day                # put its database_id in wrangler.toml
npx wrangler d1 migrations apply elastic-day --remote
npx wrangler deploy
```

Then set `VITE_PUSH_URL` in `.env.production` to the worker's address and add your site's origin to `ALLOWED_ORIGINS` in `worker/wrangler.toml`. Passkeys belong to the site they were made on, so accounts work on the origins listed there.

## Deploying the site

The app is plain static files served from `/planner/`, so it can go on any web host. `npm run deploy` runs the tests, builds, and copies `dist/` to the server with rsync over SSH. It reads the server details from a `.deploy.env` file that isn't committed:

```bash
DEPLOY_USER=you
DEPLOY_HOST=203.0.113.10
DEPLOY_PORT=22
DEPLOY_PATH=path/to/public_html/planner
```

`npm run deploy -- --dry-run` shows what would change first. To serve it from a different path, change `base` in `vite.config.ts` and the paths in `public/manifest.webmanifest`.
